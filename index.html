Criminal Law Reforms 2023
BNS · BNSS · BSA — India's new criminal justice trilogy · Effective 1 July 2024
🔴 BNS 2023
Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita
Replaces IPC 1860 → 358 Sections (was 511)
  • Terrorism explicitly defined as a separate offence for the first time in a penal code
  • Organised Crime (gang crimes, syndicate offences) codified comprehensively
  • Trial in absentia allowed — accused who flees can be convicted
  • Mob Lynching (group of 5+) made a distinct offence with death penalty
  • Snatching defined separately from theft — recognises the force element
  • Community Service introduced as a form of punishment for minor offences
  • Sedition (Sec 124A) removed — replaced with "acts endangering sovereignty"
  • Offences against women and children extensively updated and consolidated
✔ In Force: 1 July 2024
🟢 BNSS 2023
Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita
Replaces CrPC 1973 → 531 Sections (was 484)
  • Zero FIR: Register FIR at any police station, irrespective of jurisdiction
  • Mandatory notification to family/friend within 90 days of arrest
  • Electronic summons, warrants and proceedings now statutorily recognized
  • Forensic investigation mandatory for offences carrying 7+ year sentences
  • Victim must be heard before the State withdraws a case
  • Judgment must be delivered within 45 days of arguments closing
  • Witness protection scheme mandated through State Governments
  • Trial-in-absentia for proclaimed offenders with due procedural safeguards
✔ In Force: 1 July 2024
🔵 BSA 2023
Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam
Replaces Evidence Act 1872 → 170 Sections (was 167)
  • Electronic records (emails, texts, social media) admitted as primary evidence
  • Electronic/Digital Certificate under Sec 63 streamlines digital proof
  • Audio-video recordings of confessions and statements admissible
  • Joint trials across different jurisdictions explicitly codified
  • Secondary evidence provisions rationalised and expanded
  • Expert evidence (DNA, forensic reports) given clearer statutory footing
  • Removes colonial-era evidentiary assumptions, modernises the framework
✔ In Force: 1 July 2024
